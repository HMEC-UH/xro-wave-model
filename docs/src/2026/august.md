## Seasonal ENSO Forecast
El Niño conditions are currently intensifying across the tropical Pacific, with SST anomalies in the Niño3.4 region (5°S–5°N, 120°–170°W) showing a steady upward trend. The observed SST anomaly reached 2.1°C in July 2026, placing a very strong El Niño level, consistent with our previous XRO forecast from May-July 2026. Our XRO forecast Niño3.4 anomalies exceeding +3.5°C by Oct 2026, reaching peak 4.3°C by Nov-Dec 2026, providing a high level of confidence of an unprecedented super El Niño event since 1950 characterized by significant warming in the central and eastern tropical Pacific (Figure 1).

## Hawaii Wave Seasonal Outlook
The development of a super El Niño is expected to substantially modulate significant wave height (SWH) around the Hawaiian Islands over the coming seasons. Figures 2 and 3 show forecasts of seasonally mean and 90th-percentile SWH anomalies, respectively, from July-September (JAS) 2026 through early March-May (MAM) 2027. Positive SWH anomalies, indicative of enhanced wave activity, are already emerging along north- and northwest-facing shores in JAS 2026. These seasonal anomalies are expected to intensify progressively through boreal fall and winter as El Niño matures. The largest positive anomalies could exceed +0.4 m in mean SWH and +0.7 m in the 90th percentile for NDJ 2026 through JFM 2027.

The projected increase in wave heights around Hawaii is consistent with a strengthened North Pacific jet stream, an equatorward-shift of the storm track, and enhanced high-latitude swell generation, all of which are characteristics of strong El Niño winters (Stopa & Cheung, 2014; Zhao et al. 2025). In contrast, outlooks for south- and southeast-facing shores indicate weaker or near-neutral anomalies throughout the forecast period, although the forecast skill in these regions is comparatively lower.
